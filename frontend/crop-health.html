NDVI Crop Health

Normalized Difference Vegetation Index measures the health and greenness of your crops. It detects how well your crops are growing.

0.8 - 1.0 = Excellent
0.6 - 0.8 = Very Good
0.4 - 0.6 = Good
0.2 - 0.4 = Moderate
0.1 - 0.2 = Stressed
0.0 - 0.1 = Poor
< 0.0 = Critical

Higher NDVI = Healthier crops

💧

NDWI Water Stress

Normalized Difference Water Index measures the water content in your crops. It detects water stress and irrigation needs.

0.5 - 1.0 = Very High
0.3 - 0.5 = High
0.2 - 0.3 = Good
0.1 - 0.2 = Moderate
0.0 - 0.1 = Stress
-0.1 - 0.0 = High Stress
< -0.1 = Critical

Lower NDWI = Water stress

Combined Interpretation

🟢 Healthy

🟡 Water Stress

Field Health & Water Interpretation

🟢 Healthy Canopy
Robust chlorophyll & vegetative mass. NDVI: 0.6-1.0, NDWI: 0.3-0.6
🟡 Moderate Warning
Early water deficit or leaf chlorosis. NDVI: 0.3-0.5, NDWI: 0.0-0.3
🔴 Severe Anomaly
Wilting or severe nutrient starvation. NDVI: <0.3, NDWI: <0.0

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to the most common questions

What is the difference between NDVI and NDWI?
NDVI measures crop health and greenness (how healthy your crops are). NDWI measures water content and stress (how much water your crops have). Both are calculated from satellite imagery.
What do yellow areas on the map mean?
For NDVI (crop health), yellow means moderate stress from nutrients, pests, or diseases. For NDWI (water stress), yellow means mild water deficiency. Check these areas first.
What should I do if I see red areas?
Red areas indicate critical stress. For NDVI: check for pests, diseases, or severe nutrient deficiency. For NDWI: check irrigation system urgently. Take immediate action.
How often are the maps updated?
The maps are updated every 5 days using Sentinel-2 satellite imagery. You can refresh manually using the "Refresh" button.
